Memory shortcuts from the week (screenshot this)

Save this table for daily revision. These are memory aids, not rules without exceptions.

TopicMemory shortcut
Time: at / on / inAT the clock, ON the calendar day, IN the long stretch.
Place: at / on / inP-S-S: Point = AT, Surface = ON, Space = IN (general memory aid).
Rank words and preferSenior, junior, superior and inferior commonly take TO; prefer A TO B.
No extra prepositionDiscuss a topic, reach a place, enter a room; comprise is usually transitive.
Congratulate / thankCongratulate someone ON an achievement; thank someone FOR a favour.
Fill in the blanksRead the full sentence, identify the relation, check fixed pairs, then eliminate options.
Between / amongBETWEEN for distinct relationships (including more than two); AMONG within a group.
Since / forSINCE a starting point; FOR a duration.
Beside / besidesBESIDE = next to; BESIDES = in addition to.
In / intoIN often describes location; INTO often describes movement inward.
By / untilBY = no later than a deadline; UNTIL = continuing up to a time.
Look forward toLook forward TO + noun or -ING form.

Use the shortcuts to recall a rule, then check the meaning and context of each sentence.

If you have fifteen minutes, spend five minutes reviewing a shortcut and writing two sentences, five minutes revising fixed pairs, and five minutes answering a few questions. Regular focused practice is often easier to sustain than occasional long sessions.

Quick readiness check: 5 questions

Try these without checking your notes first.

  1. He was born ___ 12 May.
  2. She is fond ___ music.
  3. I have lived here ___ 2019.
  4. The prize was shared ___ the four winners. (between / among)
  5. We are looking forward ___ meeting you.
Answers: 1) on; 2) of; 3) since; 4) among (when viewed as a group); 5) to.

5 correct: keep practising and try more challenging examples.
3–4 correct: review the patterns you missed.
0–2 correct: start with the free introductory lessons.

The cost of waiting

There is no artificial deadline. Still, putting off practice can mean repeating the same avoidable mistakes: “discuss about”, “senior than” or “since five years”. Begin with a small, manageable session today rather than trying to learn everything at once.

Before buying study material, ask:

  2. Are examples provided? Can you see the rule in use?
