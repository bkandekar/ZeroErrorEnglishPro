Why prepositions deserve a dedicated ebook

Prepositions look like a small topic. They're not. Think about what examiners actually test: time and place, fixed pairs after verbs, adjectives and nouns, confusing pairs, extra prepositions, idioms and phrases. That's a lot of material hiding behind a few tiny words.

And here's the problem with learning them from random websites. One page teaches in, on and at. Another gives a list of fixed pairs. A third has a quiz with no explanation. You collect bits and pieces, but you never see the whole picture, and you never know what to revise first.

A good book solves three problems at once. It gives you a sequence, so you learn the basics before the tricky parts. It gives you practice right after each idea, so you test yourself while it's fresh. And it gives you one place to revise, so the week before the exam, you open a single book instead of twenty tabs.

Here's every shortcut from this week's posts, gathered in one table. Save it, and read it aloud once a day.

TopicMemory shortcut
Time: at / on / inAT the clock, ON the calendar day, IN the long stretch.
Place: at / on / inP-S-S: Point = AT, Surface = ON, Space = IN.
Rank words and preferSenior, junior, superior, inferior and prefer take TO, never "than".
No helper neededD-C-R-E: Discuss, Comprise, Reach, Enter go straight to their object.
congratulate / thankPodium: congratulate ON. Favour: thank FOR.
Fill in the blanksB-A-M-E-F: Be A Master, Eliminate Fast.
between / amongBeTWeen = TWo. AMONG = a MOB.
since / forNumber in front? FOR. Date or event? SINCE.
beside / besidesBeSIDE = side. BeSIDES = extra S for extra.
in / intoIN + TO = INTO.
by / untilBY = deadline. UNTIL = UNbroken stretch.
look forward to ___If TO is a preposition, -ING is the condition.

How the ebook helps you study

I teach prepositions the way I have taught them in my classroom for more than 22 years: rule, example, practice, and I wrote the ebook around the same approach. First, you see the idea in simple language. Then you see it used in real sentences. Then you test yourself with exam-style questions, so that the idea sticks.

Three principles make this method work:

  1. Families, not lists. You learn related pairs together, which your memory handles much better than a long alphabetical list.
  2. Shortcuts that stay. A tricky idea needs a way to remember it, because you don't have time to re-derive a rule in the exam hall.
  3. Practice with a purpose. Questions should follow common exam patterns, so you get used to the way examiners think.

No time? Use the 15-minute version

Not everyone can give twenty minutes a day. If you have only fifteen, split them like this: five minutes to read one shortcut and write two sentences, five minutes for ten fixed pairs, and five minutes for three or four practice questions. Fifteen focused minutes every day beat a three-hour session once a month. Consistency is what builds exam accuracy, and it works even when you're busy with college, work or other subjects.

Quick readiness check: 5 questions

Answer these without looking at anything. This is a quick check, not a test.

  1. He was born ___ 12 May.
  2. She is fond ___ music.
  3. I have lived here ___ 2019.
  4. The prize was shared ___ the four winners. (between / among)
  5. We are looking forward ___ meeting you.
Answers: 1) on 2) of 3) since 4) among 5) to

If you got all five: excellent. Keep revising with the shortcuts, and use the ebook for extra practice.
If you got three or four: you have a good base, but a few gaps remain. That's exactly where focused practice helps.
If you got two or fewer: start with this week's free posts, then move to the ebook for the full system.

How to judge any grammar book (including mine)

Before you spend money on any study material, ask these four questions. I'd want you to ask them about my ebook too.

  1. Does it explain in simple language? If you need a dictionary to understand the rule, it's the wrong book.
  2. Does it give examples for every idea? A rule without a sentence is just a slogan.
  3. Does it let you practise? Reading builds understanding. Practice builds accuracy.
  4. Does it fit your exam? A book for general English is different from a book built around exam patterns.

Open the Amazon page, read the description and judge for yourself against these four questions. That's how a smart student buys study material.
