What Are Articles, and Why Do Exams Keep Testing Them?

Articles are small words placed before a noun to show whether that noun is specific or general. English has two kinds. A and an are indefinite articles: they point to any one member of a group. The is the definite article: it points to a particular person, thing or idea that the speaker and listener both know. Sometimes a noun needs no article at all, and that case is called the zero article.

So why do exam setters like them? There are three reasons.

  • They're small, so the eye skips over them in a long sentence.
  • The rules depend on meaning and sound, not just spelling, so guessing fails.
  • Mistakes feel natural to anyone who speaks English casually. "He is best player" sounds fine until you stop and check it.

In SSC-type exams, article rules usually work behind the scenes. You may not see a question labelled "articles". You'll see a sentence split into four parts, and one part quietly needs a the.

The Three Rule Groups in Brief

1. A and An: Sound Decides, Not Spelling

Use a before a consonant sound and an before a vowel sound. Notice the word sound. The first letter is not the test.

  • an hour, an honest man, an MBA student (the h is silent, and M is pronounced "em")
  • a university, a European tour, a one-rupee coin (these begin with a "y" or "w" sound)
  • a UPSC aspirant, but an SSC aspirant

Also remember that a and an go only with singular countable nouns. You can say "a book", but not "a books" or "a water". The full breakdown, including abbreviations and tricky cases, is in our detailed guide to a and an rules with examples.

2. The: Use It When the Noun Is Specific

Use the when the noun is known, unique or clearly identified. The main cases are:

  • Something already mentioned: "I bought a phone. The phone is fast."
  • Unique things: the sun, the sky, the Constitution.
  • Superlatives and ordinals: the best, the first.
  • Rivers, oceans, seas, mountain ranges and island groups: the Ganga, the Pacific, the Himalayas.
  • A group described by an adjective: the poor, the young.

The exceptions are where the trouble starts, and they have their own space in our article on the rules of the definite article.

3. Zero Article: When No Article Is Needed

Skip the article when you speak about things in a general sense, or when the idiom demands it.

  • General plurals and uncountable nouns: "Students need practice." "Honesty pays."
  • Meals, languages and sports: have breakfast, speak Hindi, play cricket.
  • Transport and fixed phrases: by bus, at night, go to school (as a student).
  • Names of people, cities and most countries: Ravi, Pune, India.

Compare "go to school" (to study) with "go to the school" (to visit the building). The words are almost the same, but the meaning changes. That kind of contrast is easy to build into a question. You'll find the complete list in zero article rules with examples.

How Articles Appear in the SSC CGL English Section

Articles can be tested in a few common formats. The format and weightage change from year to year, so treat what follows as a practice guide, not a promise of what will appear.

Fill in the blanks

You choose from a, an, the or no article.

SSC-style example: He is ___ honest and hardworking officer.

Answer: an. Honest begins with a vowel sound because the h is silent.

Error spotting

The sentence is divided into parts, and you find the part with the error.

SSC-style example: (A) My uncle is / (B) an university / (C) professor / (D) in Mumbai.

Answer: (B). It should be "a university professor", because university begins with a "y" sound.

Sentence improvement

A part of the sentence is underlined, and you pick the best replacement.

SSC-style example: The Ganga is longest river in India.

Improved: The Ganga is the longest river in India.

Articles can also be tested inside cloze passages, where you decide from the context whether a noun is specific or general.

A 4-Step Method to Solve Any Article Question

When a sentence involves an article, don't rely on "it sounds right". Run these four checks in order.

  1. Find the noun and its type. Is it singular countable (book), plural (books), uncountable (water, advice) or a proper noun (Delhi)?
  2. Ask "Which one?" If the listener knows exactly which one, use the. If it's any one and the noun is singular countable, use a or an. If you mean the idea in general with a plural or uncountable noun, use no article.
  3. For a or an, say the next word aloud. The word right after the article matters: an old house, but a huge house. Then decide by sound.
  4. Check the exception list. Institutions (school, hospital), transport (by bus), meals and fixed phrases follow their own patterns.

Try it: He is ___ honest man and ___ university graduate.

Step 1: both nouns are singular countable. Step 2: neither is specific, so we need indefinite articles. Step 3: "honest" begins with the vowel sound "o", and "university" begins with the "y" sound. Answer: an honest man and a university graduate.

Same noun, different article, different meaning

Article choice doesn't only decide whether a sentence is correct. It can change what the sentence says. Compare these pairs.

  • "Bring me a book." Any book will do. "Bring me the book." One particular book, known to both of you.
  • "Few students passed." Almost none passed, and the tone is negative. "A few students passed." Some did, and the tone is positive.
  • "He has little hope." He is nearly hopeless. "He has a little hope." He still has some.

Pairs like the last two are worth memorising, because a wrong article there doesn't just look untidy. It says the opposite of what you meant.

8 Common Traps in Article Questions

Trap 1: Choosing by spelling instead of sound

IncorrectShe is a honest girl.

CorrectShe is an honest girl.

Why: honest begins with a vowel sound because the h is silent.

Trap 2: Missing the "w" and "y" sounds

IncorrectIt was an one-day match.

CorrectIt was a one-day match.

Why: "one" begins with a "w" sound, which is a consonant sound. Words like university and European work the same way with a "y" sound.

Trap 3: Dropping the with superlatives

IncorrectHe is best player in the team.

CorrectHe is the best player in the team.

Why: a superlative points to one particular top member, so it needs the.

Trap 4: Adding the before a general idea

IncorrectThe honesty is the best policy.

CorrectHonesty is the best policy.

Why: honesty here is a general idea, not a particular instance of it.

Trap 5: Using an article with meals

IncorrectI had the breakfast at eight.

CorrectI had breakfast at eight.

Why: meals usually take no article. The exception is a specific meal: "The breakfast at the hotel was excellent."

Trap 6: Mishandling institutions and transport

IncorrectShe goes to the school by the bus.

CorrectShe goes to school by bus.

Why: "school" here means studying, not the building, and "by" plus a mode of transport takes no article.

Trap 7: Stacking two determiners

IncorrectI lost the my pen.

CorrectI lost my pen.

Why: words like my, this and that already do the work of an article, so the two can't sit together.

Trap 8: Using a with an uncountable noun

IncorrectShe gave me a good advice.

CorrectShe gave me good advice.

Why: advice is uncountable. If you want a countable form, say "a piece of advice". Information, furniture and luggage behave the same way.

10 SSC-Style Practice Questions

Set a timer for six minutes and try all ten before you open the answers. Choose from (A) a, (B) an, (C) the, (D) no article, unless the options say otherwise.

  1. She bought ___ umbrella because it was raining.
  2. He is ___ honest officer.
  3. ___ Ganga flows through several states.
  4. ___ Himalayas protect northern India from cold winds.
  5. I usually have ___ breakfast at 8 a.m.
  6. He goes to ___ school by ___ bus. (A) the / the   (B) no article / no article   (C) a / the   (D) the / no article
  7. Ravi is ___ best batsman in our college team.
  8. She gave me ___ useful advice about my career.
  9. She visited ___ European country last summer.
  10. ___ more you practise, ___ better you become. (A) The / the   (B) A / the   (C) The / a   (D) No article / the
Show answers and explanations
  1. (B) an. Umbrella begins with a vowel sound.
  2. (B) an. Honest has a silent h, so the sound is a vowel sound.
  3. (C) The. Names of rivers take the.
  4. (C) The. Mountain ranges take the.
  5. (D) no article. Meals take no article in a general sense.
  6. (B) no article / no article. "Go to school" means going as a student, and "by bus" takes no article.
  7. (C) the. A superlative needs the.
  8. (D) no article. Advice is uncountable, so a and an are out, and no particular advice is being identified.
  9. (A) a. European begins with a "y" sound, a consonant sound.
  10. (A) The / the. The pattern "the more..., the better..." uses the twice.

If you missed more than three, don't worry. Note which rule caused each miss. That list will guide your revision better than doing another random set. For more timed sets, see our page of articles questions for SSC CGL with exam pattern and practice.

Quick Revision and a 7-Day Plan

Quick revision box

  • A / an: decide by the sound of the next word. Use them only with singular countable nouns.
  • The: known nouns, unique things, superlatives, ordinals, rivers, oceans, seas and mountain ranges.
  • No article: general plurals, uncountable nouns, meals, languages, and "by" plus transport.
  • Never place two determiners together (the my, a this).
  • Uncountable nouns such as advice, information and furniture never take a or an.
  • Institutions such as school and hospital lose the article when they mean the purpose, not the building.

A simple 7-day plan

  • Day 1: Learn the a / an sound rule. Write ten sentences of your own with tricky words.
  • Day 2: Study the rules of the. Write two sentences for each rule.
  • Day 3: Study zero article, school and hospital phrases, and transport phrases.
  • Day 4: Solve 20 fill-in-the-blank questions against the clock.
  • Day 5: Solve 15 error spotting questions and note every wrong answer.
  • Day 6: Try sentence improvement and one mixed set. Revisit your error notes.
  • Day 7: Redo every missed question and re-read the eight traps.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What are the rules of articles for SSC CGL?

A: The rules fall into three groups. Use a or an with singular countable nouns, choosing by sound. Use the with specific or known nouns, superlatives, ordinals, rivers and similar names. Use no article with general plurals, uncountable nouns, meals, languages and "by" plus transport. Then learn the exceptions.

Q: When do we use "the"?

A: Use the when the noun is clearly identified: it was mentioned before, it is unique, or it is made specific by a phrase such as "of the school". It also goes with superlatives, ordinal numbers and names like the Ganga and the Himalayas.

Q: When is no article used?

A: No article is used with plural and uncountable nouns in a general sense ("Books are useful"), with meals, languages and sports, with most names of people and places, and in fixed phrases such as "by train" and "at night".

Q: What is the difference between "a" and "an"?

A: Both mean "one of many". The only difference is sound. A comes before a consonant sound and an before a vowel sound, so we say "an hour" but "a university".

Q: How can I solve article questions in SSC CGL?

A: Follow the four-step method: identify the noun type, ask whether it's specific or general, check the sound for a or an, and test the exception list. Then practise under time pressure and keep a log of your mistakes so you can see which rule needs work.

Q: What are the common article errors in competitive exams?

A: The most common ones are choosing a or an by spelling, dropping the before superlatives, adding the before general ideas, using a or an with uncountable nouns, and mishandling phrases such as "go to school" and "by bus".
