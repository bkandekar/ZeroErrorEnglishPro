Punctuation Error Spotting for SSC CGL and Bank Exams: Tricks, Examples and Practice

Master punctuation error spotting for SSC CGL, CHSL and bank exams with clear rules, memory tricks, common traps, examples and a full practice set with answers.

Core Governing Rule Formula:
COMMA pause & list | APOSTROPHE ownership or contraction | QUOTES exact words | COLON introduce | SEMICOLON join equals | FULL STOP end the thought

every lost mark.</p> <p><b>Reason 1: we hear the pause, not the mark.</b> When we read “The students who passed the test were called for counselling,” we naturally pause after “test.” Our ear accepts a comma there, even though the relative clause is defining and needs no comma.</p> <p><b>Reason 2: we copy spoken English.</b> In spoken English we say “Its raining” and “The boys books.” The apostrophe disappears in speech, so we forget it on paper. Examiners plant exactly these gaps.</p> <p>The cure is the same as with fixed prepositions: stop trusting the sound. Treat each mark as a fixed rule attached to a structure, the way you treat “depend on” as one unit.</p> <h2 id="section-trap">Five common Indian-language traps</h2> <p>Most punctuation mistakes in Indian exam papers come from habits formed by Hindi, Marathi or other regional languages. Here are the five patterns that appear most often.</p> <table> <tr><th>What the mind wants</th><th>What it writes</th><th>What English requires</th></tr> <tr><td>Pause after a long subject</td><td><span class="zp-no">The candidates who scored highest, were selected.</span></td><td><span class="zp-yes">No comma</span> (defining clause)</td></tr> <tr><td>Ownership without mark</td><td><span class="zp-no">The students books</span></td><td><span class="zp-yes">The students’ books</span></td></tr> <tr><td>Quotation without closing mark</td><td><span class="zp-no">He said, “I will come tomorrow.</span></td><td><span class="zp-yes">… tomorrow.”</span></td></tr> <tr><td>List with “and” and extra comma</td><td><span class="zp-no">books, pens, and pencils,</span></td><td><span class="zp-yes">books, pens and pencils</span> (or Oxford comma, but consistent)</td></tr> <tr><td>Its / It’s confusion</td><td><span class="zp-no">Its raining outside.</span></td><td><span class="zp-yes">It’s raining</span> (contraction)</td></tr> </table> <p>Once you see the pattern, the mark stops being optional. It becomes a fixed requirement of the structure.</p> <h2 id="section-map">The 6-box punctuation map</h2> <p>Six boxes cover every punctuation item that SSC and bank papers actually test. Name the box before you decide the mark.</p> <div class="zp-trick"> <p style="margin:0"> <b>Box 1: COMMA</b> = pause, list, non-defining clause<br> <b>Box 2: APOSTROPHE</b> = ownership or missing letter<br> <b>Box 3: QUOTATION MARKS</b> = exact words spoken or written<br> <b>Box 4: COLON</b> = introduce a list or explanation<br> <b>Box 5: SEMICOLON</b> = join two equal independent clauses<br> <b>Box 6: FULL STOP / QUESTION / EXCLAMATION</b> = end the thought correctly</p> </div> <p>Before you mark an option wrong, ask: which box does this sentence belong to? That single question removes most guesswork.</p> <h2 id="section-rules">The six groups with examples</h2> <h3>Box 1: Comma – the most tested mark</h3> <div class="zp-ex"> <b>1. No comma in defining relative clauses</b> <span class="zp-yes">✓ The candidates who scored above 160 were selected.</span> <span class="zp-no">✗ The candidates, who scored above 160, were selected.</span><br> <b>2. Comma in non-defining clauses</b> <span class="zp-yes">✓ My brother, who lives in Pune, is an engineer.</span> <span class="zp-no">✗ My brother who lives in Pune is an engineer.</span> (when there is only one brother)<br> <b>3. Comma after introductory phrases</b> <span class="zp-yes">✓ After finishing the paper, she left the hall.</span> <span class="zp-no">✗ After finishing the paper she left the hall.</span><br> <b>4. Comma in lists</b> <span class="zp-yes">✓ She bought books, pens and notebooks.</span> (Oxford comma optional but consistency matters)<br> <b>5. No comma between subject and verb</b> <span class="zp-yes">✓ The results of the examination were declared yesterday.</span> <span class="zp-no">✗ The results of the examination, were declared yesterday.</span> </div> <p><b>Memory trick:</b> “Defining = no pause, non-defining = two commas.” If the clause is essential to identify the noun, drop the commas. If it is extra information, wrap it in commas.</p> <h3>Box 2: Apostrophe – ownership or contraction</h3> <div class="zp-ex"> <b>6. Singular ownership</b> <span class="zp-yes">✓ The girl’s bag was missing.</span> <span class="zp-no">✗ The girls bag was missing.</span><br> <b>7. Plural ownership (already ending in s)</b> <span class="zp-yes">✓ The girls’ bags were missing.</span> <span class="zp-no">✗ The girl’s bags were missing.</span> (when more than one girl)<br> <b>8. Its vs It’s</b> <span class="zp-yes">✓ It’s raining. The dog wagged its tail.</span> <span class="zp-no">✗ Its raining. The dog wagged it’s tail.</span><br> <b>9. Whose vs Who’s</b> <span class="zp-yes">✓ Whose book is this? Who’s coming with us?</span><br> <b>10. Years and decades</b> <span class="zp-yes">✓ The 1990s were memorable. / She was born in the ’90s.</span> </div> <p><b>Memory trick:</b> “Apostrophe = something missing or belonging.” If a letter is missing (it is → it’s), use the apostrophe. If ownership is shown, place the apostrophe before or after the s according to singular or plural.</p> <h3>Box 3: Quotation marks</h3> <div class="zp-ex"> <b>11. Exact words</b> <span class="zp-yes">✓ He said, “I will submit the form tomorrow.”</span><br> <b>12. Closing mark and punctuation</b> <span class="zp-yes">✓ “Are you ready?” she asked.</span> <span class="zp-no">✗ “Are you ready”? she asked.</span><br> <b>13. Titles of short works</b> <span class="zp-yes">✓ I read the poem “If” by Kipling.</span> </div> <p><b>Memory trick:</b> “Quotes = exact words only.” Do not put quotes around reported speech that has been changed. Always close the marks, and place the final punctuation inside the closing quotation mark in most exam styles.</p> <h3>Box 4: Colon</h3> <div class="zp-ex"> <b>14. Introduce a list</b> <span class="zp-yes">✓ She packed three things: a notebook, a pen and a calculator.</span><br> <b>15. Introduce an explanation or example</b> <span class="zp-yes">✓ He had one goal: to clear SSC CGL in the first attempt.</span> </div> <p><b>Memory trick:</b> “Colon = announcement.” What comes before the colon must be a complete independent clause. Never use a colon after a verb or preposition that already introduces the list.</p> <h3>Box 5: Semicolon</h3> <div class="zp-ex"> <b>16. Join two related independent clauses</b> <span class="zp-yes">✓ The paper was tough; many candidates left questions unanswered.</span> <span class="zp-no">✗ The paper was tough, many candidates left questions unanswered.</span><br> <b>17. Before conjunctive adverbs</b> <span class="zp-yes">✓ She studied hard; therefore, she scored well.</span> </div> <p><b>Memory trick:</b> “Semicolon = equal partners.” Both sides must be able to stand alone as sentences. If one side is a fragment, use a comma or rewrite.</p> <h3>Box 6: Full stop, question mark, exclamation</h3> <div class="zp-ex"> <b>18. Full stop ends a statement</b> <span class="zp-yes">✓ The results will be declared next week.</span><br> <b>19. Question mark for direct questions only</b> <span class="zp-yes">✓ Did you submit the form?</span> <span class="zp-no">✗ He asked did you submit the form?</span><br> <b>20. Exclamation for strong feeling</b> <span class="zp-yes">✓ What a brilliant performance!</span> </div> <p><b>Memory trick:</b> “End the thought with the right signal.” A reported question takes a full stop, not a question mark. An indirect statement never takes a question mark.</p> <h2 id="section-table">Quick reference table</h2> <p>Screenshot this for your revision folder.</p> <table> <tr><th>Box</th><th>Key rule</th><th>Watch for</th></tr> <tr><td>COMMA</td><td>Defining = no comma; non-defining = commas</td><td>Extra comma between subject and verb</td></tr> <tr><td>APOSTROPHE</td><td>Ownership or contraction</td><td>its / it’s, whose / who’s</td></tr> <tr><td>QUOTES</td><td>Exact words only; close the marks</td><td>Missing closing quote</td></tr> <tr><td>COLON</td><td>Introduce list or explanation</td><td>Colon after incomplete clause</td></tr> <tr><td>SEMICOLON</td><td>Join two independent clauses</td><td>Comma splice</td></tr> <tr><td>END MARKS</td><td>Statement / question / feeling</td><td>Question mark on reported speech</td></tr> </table> <h2 id="section-pairs">Pairs that examiners love</h2> <div class="zp-box"> <p><b>its / it’s</b> — its = belonging; it’s = it is / it has.</p> <p><b>whose / who’s</b> — whose = belonging; who’s = who is / who has.</p> <p><b>defining / non-defining</b> — essential information = no commas; extra information = commas.</p> <p><b>comma splice / semicolon</b> — two independent clauses joined only by a comma is wrong; use semicolon or full stop.</p> <p><b>direct / reported question</b> — direct takes ? ; reported takes full stop.</p> </div> <h2 id="section-mistakes">8 high-frequency mistakes in exams</h2> <div class="zp-box"> <p><b>1. Extra comma after long subject.</b> <span class="zp-no">✗ The students who cleared the cut-off, were called for document verification.</span> <span class="zp-yes">✓ … cut-off were called …</span></p> <p><b>2. Missing apostrophe in plural ownership.</b> <span class="zp-no">✗ The teachers room is on the first floor.</span> <span class="zp-yes">✓ The teachers’ room …</span></p> <p><b>3. It’s vs its.</b> <span class="zp-no">✗ The committee has submitted it’s report.</span> <span class="zp-yes">✓ … its report.</span></p> <p><b>4. Comma splice.</b> <span class="zp-no">✗ The paper was lengthy, many left it incomplete.</span> <span class="zp-yes">✓ … lengthy; many left …</span></p> <p><b>5. Question mark on reported speech.</b> <span class="zp-no">✗ He asked me where was I going?</span> <span class="zp-yes">✓ … where I was going.</span></p> <p><b>6. Missing closing quotation mark.</b> <span class="zp-no">✗ She said, “I have finished the syllabus.</span> <span class="zp-yes">✓ … syllabus.”</span></p> <p><b>7. Colon after a verb.</b> <span class="zp-no">✗ She wants: a high score and a good rank.</span> <span class="zp-yes">✓ She wants a high score and a good rank. / She has two aims: a high score and a good rank.</span></p> <p><b>8. Apostrophe in ordinary plurals.</b> <span class="zp-no">✗ The 1990’s were important.</span> <span class="zp-yes">✓ The 1990s were important.</span></p> </div> <h2 id="section-method">The 5-step punctuation check</h2> <div class="zp-box"> <p><b>Step 1: Spot every mark and every missing mark.</b> Underline commas, apostrophes, quotes, colons, semicolons and end marks.</p> <p><b>Step 2: Name the box.</b> Is this a comma issue, an apostrophe issue, a quote issue, etc.?</p> <p><b>Step 3: Apply the core rule of that box.</b> Defining or non-defining? Ownership or contraction? Exact words or reported speech?</p> <p><b>Step 4: Check the pairs.</b> its/it’s, whose/who’s, defining/non-defining, comma splice.</p> <p><b>Step 5: Read the sentence without the mark.</b> If the meaning collapses or becomes ambiguous, the mark is required; if the meaning stays clear and the clause is defining, remove the extra mark.</p> </div> <h2 id="section-plan">The 5-day revision plan</h2> <p>Twenty minutes a day is enough.</p> <div class="zp-box"> <p><b>Day 1: Comma rules.</b> Write five defining and five non-defining sentences. Practise removing the forbidden subject-verb comma.</p> <p><b>Day 2: Apostrophe.</b> Make a two-column list: ownership vs contraction. Write ten its/it’s and whose/who’s sentences.</p> <p><b>Day 3: Quotes, colon, semicolon.</b> Practise direct speech, lists after colons, and two independent clauses joined by semicolon.</p> <p><b>Day 4: End marks and reported speech.</b> Convert ten direct questions into reported speech and remove the question mark.</p> <p><b>Day 5: Mixed practice.</b> Solve the practice set below under timed conditions. Note every error and revisit that box.</p> </div> <h2 id="section-practice">Practice set: error spotting</h2> <p>These are original practice sentences written for this guide.</p> <h3>Round 1: spot the error</h3> <p>Each sentence has one punctuation error. Identify and correct it.</p> <ol> <li>The candidates who scored the highest marks, were called for the interview.</li> <li>Its important to revise the notes daily.</li> <li>She said, “I have completed the syllabus.</li> <li>The paper was difficult, many students could not finish it.</li> <li>My brother who lives in Delhi is a doctor.</li> <li>The teachers room is on the second floor.</li> <li>He asked me where was I going?</li> <li>She has three goals a government job, a stable income and respect.</li> </ol> <div class="zp-box"> <p><b>Answers</b></p> <p>1. Remove the comma after marks (defining clause). &nbsp; 2. It’s important … (contraction). &nbsp; 3. Close the quotation: … syllabus.” &nbsp; 4. Use semicolon or full stop: … difficult; many students … &nbsp; 5. Add commas if non-defining: My brother, who lives in Delhi, is a doctor. &nbsp; 6. teachers’ room (plural ownership). &nbsp; 7. Remove question mark and invert: … where I was going. &nbsp; 8. Add colon: … three goals: a government job …</p> </div> <h3>Round 2: fill in the correct mark</h3> <p>Choose the correct punctuation or write “none”.</p> <ol> <li>The results of the examination ___ were declared yesterday. (comma / none)</li> <li>The dog wagged ___ tail happily. (its / it’s)</li> <li>“Will you join us tomorrow___” she asked. (? / .)</li> <li>He studied hard___ therefore he scored well. (; / ,)</li> <li>Whose / Who’s bag is lying on the bench?</li> <li>She wants only one thing___ selection in the first attempt. (: / ,)</li> <li>The 1990___ were a decade of change. (s / ’s)</li> <li>After submitting the form___ she left the centre. (, / none)</li> </ol> <div class="zp-box"> <p><b>Answers</b></p> <p>1. none &nbsp; 2. its &nbsp; 3. ? &nbsp; 4. ; &nbsp; 5. Whose &nbsp; 6. : &nbsp; 7. s &nbsp; 8. ,</p> </div> <p>Scored 7 or more? You are exam-ready on punctuation. Below 5? Revisit Boxes 1 and 2 for two more days.</p> <h2 id="section-faq">Frequently asked questions</h2> <h3>Is the Oxford comma compulsory in SSC and bank exams?</h3> <p>No. Both “A, B and C” and “A, B, and C” are accepted provided the paper is consistent. The error usually lies in a missing or extra comma that changes meaning, not in the presence or absence of the final serial comma.</p> <h3>How many punctuation questions appear in SSC CGL Tier-1?</h3> <p>Usually 0–2 direct questions, but punctuation errors are frequently embedded inside error-spotting and sentence-improvement questions. Treating them as a separate skill pays off.</p> <h3>Should I use a semicolon or a full stop between two independent clauses?</h3> <p>Both are correct. The semicolon shows a closer relationship between the ideas; the full stop is safer if you are unsure.</p> <h3>Is “it’s” ever used for possession?</h3> <p>Never. “It’s” is only the contraction of “it is” or “it has.” Possession is always “its” without an apostrophe.</p> <h3>What is the fastest way to improve?</h3> <p>Practise the 5-step check on every error-spotting set you solve. Five focused days on the six boxes remove most of the recurring mistakes.</p> <h2>Your next step</h2> <p>Punctuation rewards precision, not intuition.
