Articles (A, An, The) Shortcut Rules for SSC, Railway and Bank Exams

Learn 10 article shortcuts for a, an and the with memory hooks, exam-style examples and a practice set. Built for SSC, railway and bank exams.

Core Governing Rule Formula:
Say the next sound -> Sort the noun -> See if it is specific

Try this in ten seconds: (A) He cleared / (B) a SSC exam / (C) last year. / (D) No error.

Most students read it, see a consonant letter and pick (D). But the rule for a and an follows sound, not spelling. The letter S is pronounced “ess,” which starts with a vowel sound, so the sentence needs “an SSC exam.” The very name of your exam hides a trap, and examiners know it.

That’s the nature of article questions. They look like the smallest things in the sentence, and that’s exactly why eyes skip them. A, an and the can’t be reasoned out from meaning alone. They follow short patterns, and once you carry those patterns in your head, the whole section becomes free marks.

This guide gives you 10 article shortcuts, each with a memory hook, a wrong sentence and its correction. You’ll also get a three-word routine called SAY, SORT, SEE, a 10-second time plan and a nine-question practice set with answers. How articles are tested in exams

Articles appear in SSC, railway and bank papers in several disguises. Knowing the formats tells you where to look.

1. Error spotting. A sentence is split into parts (A), (B), (C), and (D) is “No error.” The article sits at the start of a part, so it’s easy to read past.

2. Fill in the blanks. The options are a, an, the and no article. Only one forms the right pattern with the noun.

3. Cloze passages. A paragraph with several blanks, where articles are mixed with prepositions and verbs.

A note on the examples in this post: every sentence is an original practice sentence on a common exam pattern. None of them is copied from a particular year’s paper, and I haven’t attached any year to them. The patterns are what repeat, and the patterns are what you should learn.

The SAY, SORT, SEE routine

You can’t recall a long rulebook with the clock running. You need one routine that works on every article question.

“SAY, SORT, SEE.”

SAY the first sound of the word after the article: vowel sound or consonant sound?
SORT the noun: countable, uncountable, or the name of a place or thing?
SEE whether the noun is specific or already mentioned: then the, otherwise a, an or no article

Say the three words silently on every sentence. It takes about five seconds, and it stops you from choosing an article by how it sounds.

10 article shortcuts with examples

Trick 1: Say the Letter

✗ He cleared a SSC exam last year.
✓ He cleared an SSC exam last year.

For abbreviations, say the letter name and listen to its first sound. Letters like S, M, R, N, L, F and H start with a vowel sound, so they take an. Memory trick: “Say the letter, hear the sound.” So it is an RRB, an NDA, an MPSC and an LLB, but a UPSC, a CBSE and a PO.

Trick 2: Flowing, Wide or in a Row: The

✗ We crossed Ganga by boat.
✓ We crossed the Ganga by boat.

Rivers, oceans, seas and mountain ranges take the. Single mountains, lakes and continents don’t, as in Mount Everest and Lake Victoria. Memory trick: “Flowing, wide or in a row: The. Single and still: bare.” The Himalayas, the Pacific Ocean and the Nile all take the.

Trick 3: Plural or Union: The

✗ He lives in Netherlands now.
✓ He lives in the Netherlands now.

Names of countries that are plural or unions take the: the Netherlands, the Philippines, the USA, the UK, the UAE. Single-name countries like India and Nepal don’t. Memory trick: “Plural or union: The.”

Trick 4: A Group Needs The

✗ Rich should help poor.
✓ The rich should help the poor.

Use the before an adjective to name a whole group of people: the rich, the poor, the young, the elderly. The same goes for nationalities such as the French. Memory trick: “A group needs The.”

Trick 5: Such, What, Many, Quite: A Follows

✗ He is a such good boy.
✓ He is such a good boy.

After such, what, many and quite, the article comes after the word. Likewise half an hour, not a half hour. Memory trick: “Such, What, Many, Quite: A comes after.” So you say “What a day!” and “Many a student has failed.”

Trick 6: Uncountables Hate A and An

✗ She gave me an advice yesterday.
✓ She gave me a piece of advice yesterday.

Advice, information, furniture, luggage, knowledge, news and bread are uncountable, so they never take a or an. Say some advice or a piece of advice. Memory trick: “If you can’t count it, a can’t join it.” You’ll see this in zero article rules.

Trick 7: Same, Following, Former, Latter: The Leads

✗ We study in same class.
✓ We study in the same class.

The goes before same, following, former and latter, and before next or last when they point to a specific one. Memory trick: “Same and following always follow The.”

Trick 8: First A, Then The

✗ I bought a book. A book was interesting.
✓ I bought a book. The book was interesting.

A noun mentioned for the first time takes a or an. When you mention it again, it becomes specific, so it takes the. Memory trick: “First a, then the.”

Trick 9: Games Are Bare, Instruments Wear The

✗ She plays guitar very well.
✓ She plays the guitar very well.

Musical instruments take the, while games and sports take no article (plays cricket, plays chess). Memory trick: “Games are bare. Instruments wear The.”

Trick 10: Purpose Is Bare, Building Is The

✗ The child goes to the school every day.
✓ The child goes to school every day.

When you go to school, college, hospital, church or bed for their usual purpose, use no article. When you mean the building itself, use the. Memory trick: “Purpose is bare. Building is The.” So “He went to the school to meet the principal” is correct, because the visit is to the building.

Three trap sentences examiners reuse

Once you know the ten shortcuts, these three disguises are easy to spot.

Trap 1: The abbreviation. “He is a MBA from Pune.” M is pronounced “em,” so use “an MBA.”

Trap 2: The single peak. “She climbed the Mount Everest.” A single mountain takes no article, so use “Mount Everest.”

Trap 3: The uncountable noun. “I need an information about the exam.” Information is uncountable, so use “some information.”

Notice the pattern. Each sentence sounds smooth, and the error is a single small word. That’s why SAY comes first. Pronounce the next word before you trust the article. More patterns live in articles error spotting.

Say each one in a full sentence: “an SSC officer,” “a UPSC aspirant.” Your ear will start to catch the wrong one.

The 5-step error spotting method

SAY, SORT, SEE tells you what to do. This routine tells you how to run it from start to finish.

Step 1: Read the sentence once. Circle every article.

Step 2: SAY. Pronounce the word after each article and listen for a vowel or consonant sound.

Step 3: SORT. Decide if the noun is countable, uncountable or a name.

Step 4: SEE. Check if it’s specific or already mentioned, and test your fix by reading the corrected sentence.

Step 5: Only then choose “No error.” It’s a conclusion, never a default.

A 10-second time plan

Here’s a plan that works for most students. Adjust the seconds to suit your pace.

0 to 2 seconds: read once and circle the articles.

2 to 5 seconds: SAY the next word and SORT the noun.

5 to 8 seconds: SEE if it’s specific, and CHECK the article.

8 to 10 seconds: mark the answer and move on. If you’re stuck, skip it and return.

A pattern I see in almost every batch

Here’s something that repeats year after year in my classes. A student can recite “use an before a vowel sound” perfectly, then picks “No error” on “a SSC exam.” The rule is there. The sound isn’t.

The fix is a habit, not more theory. Take ten sentences from any newspaper, circle every article and say the next word aloud. Ten sentences a day for two weeks trains your ear to catch the sound before your eye trusts the spelling, and that’s the whole skill. For more practice, try the articles practice questions.

Expert tips for exam day

Say it, don’t read it. Mouth the word after the article, and the sound will tell you.

Trust the pattern over your ear. “It sounds fine” is why most article errors survive.

Check the noun, not just the article. Many errors are really countable and uncountable errors.

Keep an error log. Write down every pattern you miss and revise that list for a week.

Three mistakes to avoid

Judging by spelling. The sound decides a or an. Letters don’t.

Using the wrong trick. Each shortcut has a trigger. Check that the trigger is really in the sentence.

Learning all ten in one evening. It feels productive and fades by morning. Two a day holds far better.

Practice set: 9 error spotting questions

Find the part with the error, or choose (D) if there’s none. These are original practice sentences written for this guide. Try them before you read the answers.

  1. He cleared (A) / a SSC exam (B) / last year. (C) / No error (D)
  2. We crossed (A) / Ganga (B) / by boat. (C) / No error (D)
  3. He lives (A) / in Netherlands (B) / now. (C) / No error (D)
  4. Rich should (A) / help the poor (B) / always. (C) / No error (D)
  5. He is (A) / a such (B) / good boy. (C) / No error (D)
  6. She gave me (A) / an advice (B) / yesterday. (C) / No error (D)
  7. She plays (A) / guitar (B) / very well. (C) / No error (D)
  8. The child goes (A) / to the school (B) / every day. (C) / No error (D)
  9. I bought a book. (A) / The book (B) / was interesting. (C) / No error (D)

Answers

1. (B): S is pronounced “ess,” so use “an SSC exam.” (Shortcut 1)

2. (B): a river takes the, so use “the Ganga.” (Shortcut 2)

3. (B): the Netherlands is plural, so use “in the Netherlands.” (Shortcut 3)

4. (A): a group needs the, so use “The rich should.” (Shortcut 4)

5. (B): the article follows such, so use “such a.” (Shortcut 5)

6. (B): advice is uncountable, so use “some advice” or “a piece of advice.” (Shortcut 6)

7. (B): an instrument takes the, so use “the guitar.” (Shortcut 9)

8. (B): purpose is bare, so use “to school.” (Shortcut 10)

9. (D): no error. The first mention takes a, and the second takes the. (Shortcut 8)

If you scored 8 or 9, you’re ready for harder questions. If you scored 5 to 7, revise the shortcuts you missed. If you scored below 5, go back to SAY, SORT, SEE and the cheat sheet, and retake the set after two days. For one more rule set, see the rules for the definite article.

Frequently asked questions

How do I decide between a and an?

Listen to the first sound of the next word, not its spelling. Use an before a vowel sound, as in an hour and an SSC exam, and a before a consonant sound, as in a university and a UPSC aspirant.

Which countries take the?

Plural names and unions take the, such as the Netherlands, the Philippines, the USA, the UK and the UAE. Single-name countries like India and Nepal don’t.

Do rivers and mountains take the?

Rivers, oceans, seas and mountain ranges take the, as in the Ganga and the Himalayas. A single mountain such as Mount Everest takes no article.

Why can’t I say an advice?

Advice is an uncountable noun, so it can’t take a or an. Say some advice or a piece of advice.

How many article questions come in SSC, railway and bank exams?

There’s no fixed number, because articles appear in error spotting, fill in the blanks and cloze questions. Check the latest official notification for your exam pattern.
