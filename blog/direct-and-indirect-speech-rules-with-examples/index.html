Direct and Indirect Speech Rules with Examples: A Simple Guide for Competitive Exams

Quick answer: how to convert direct speech into indirect speech
  1. Remove the quotation marks and add that (for statements).
  2. Change the pronouns to match the speaker and the listener.
  3. Move the tense one step back.
  4. Change time and place words (now to then, here to there).
  5. Change said to into told.
Example: He said, "I am tired." becomes He said that he was tired.

What are direct and indirect speech?

Direct speech gives the exact words of the speaker, inside quotation marks. Indirect speech (also called reported speech) tells us what the speaker said, without quotation marks and with some changes.

  • Direct: Rahul said, "I like cricket."
  • Indirect: Rahul said that he liked cricket.

Every direct speech sentence has two parts. The first is the reporting clause, which tells us who is speaking (Rahul said). The second is the reported speech, which is the exact words (I like cricket). When we convert, the reporting clause stays almost as it is, and the reported speech changes.

Exams test this topic in several forms: converting a sentence from direct to indirect or the other way, choosing the correct option, and spotting errors in a reported sentence. All of them rely on the same rules.

The 5-step method (works for every statement)

Let's convert this sentence together: Meera said to her friend, "I will send you the notes tomorrow."

StepWhat you doResult
1Find the reporting verb and the reported speechReporting verb: said to. Reported speech: I will send you the notes tomorrow.
2Remove quotes, add "that", change said to into toldMeera told her friend that ...
3Change the pronounsI becomes she. You becomes her.
4Move the tense one step backwill send becomes would send
5Change the time wordtomorrow becomes the next day

Final answer: Meera told her friend that she would send her the notes the next day.

Practise this routine on five or six sentences, and it will start to feel automatic. Now let's look at each rule properly.

Rule 1: The reporting verb

If the reporting verb is said with no listener, it stays said. If it's said to (with a listener), change it to told.

  • He said, "I am ready." becomes He said that he was ready.
  • He said to me, "I am ready." becomes He told me that he was ready.

Examiners love this one. "He said me that he was ready" is wrong, and so is "He told that he was ready". Remember: said goes straight to "that", while told always needs a person after it.

Rule 2: Pronoun changes

Pronouns change according to who is speaking and who is listening. The rule has three parts:

  • First person (I, we, my, our) changes according to the subject of the reporting verb.
  • Second person (you, your) changes according to the object of the reporting verb.
  • Third person (he, she, they, it) doesn't change.

Example: Anil said to Sunil, "I will help you."

"I" is first person, so it follows the subject: Anil becomes he. "You" is second person, so it follows the object: Sunil becomes him. Result: Anil told Sunil that he would help him.

Memory shortcut: S-O-N. First person follows the Subject. Second person follows the Object. Third person has No change.

Rule 3: Tense changes (one step back)

When the reporting verb is in the past (said, told), the verb in the reported speech moves one step back into the past. I'll cover the full chart in the next post of this series. Here's the quick version.

Direct speechIndirect speech
Present simple (He plays)Past simple (He played)
Present continuous (He is playing)Past continuous (He was playing)
Present perfect (He has played)Past perfect (He had played)
Past simple (He played)Past perfect (He had played)
will, can, maywould, could, might
Memory shortcut: ONE STEP BACK. Think of reversing a car. Each tense takes one step back, and "will" becomes "would" because it steps back too.

Rule 4: Time and place words

Words that point to the present moment or the present place also change, because the situation has moved on.

Memory shortcut: N-F (Near to Far). Near words like now, here and this become far words like then, there and that.

When the tense does NOT change

Rule 3 has exceptions, and examiners know that students forget them. Here are the four main ones.

1. The reporting verb is in the present or future tense

  • He says, "I am busy." becomes He says that he is busy.
  • She will say, "I need help." becomes She will say that she needs help.

Only the pronoun changes. The tense stays the same, because the report is happening now.

2. The reported speech is a universal truth or habitual fact

  • The teacher said, "The sun rises in the east." becomes The teacher said that the sun rises in the east.
  • He said, "Honesty is the best policy." becomes He said that honesty is the best policy.

A fact that's always true doesn't take a step back. Memory shortcut: frozen facts don't move.

3. The verb is already in the past perfect tense

  • He said, "I had finished the work." becomes He said that he had finished the work.

There's no step further back to take, so the past perfect stays as it is.

4. Certain modals don't change

Would, could, should, might, ought to and used to stay unchanged. "She said, 'I could swim well.'" becomes "She said that she could swim well." The modal must usually stays too, although "had to" is also accepted: "He said, 'I must go.'" becomes "He said that he must go" or "he had to go".

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between direct and indirect speech?

Direct speech repeats the exact words of the speaker inside quotation marks. Indirect speech reports the meaning in the reporter's own words, without quotation marks, and with changes in tense, pronouns and time words.

Why does the tense change in indirect speech?

The tense moves one step back because the original words were spoken in the past. When the reporting verb is in the past, the reported speech shifts back to match.

When should I use "said" and when "told"?

Use "said" when there's no listener, and "said to" with a listener becomes "told". "He told me that..." is correct, and "He said me that..." is wrong.

Does the tense always change in indirect speech?

No. It doesn't change if the reporting verb is in the present or future tense, if the statement is a universal truth or habitual fact, if the verb is already in the past perfect, or if the modal is would, could, should, might or ought to.

Is "that" always needed in indirect speech?

For statements, "that" is the usual connector, and in exams you should include it. In everyday speech it's sometimes dropped, but safe exam practice is to write it.

How can I remember all the rules?

Use the shortcuts: ONE STEP BACK for tenses, S-O-N for pronouns and N-F for time and place words. Say them aloud, and practise ten sentences a day for a week.
