Apostrophe Rules with Examples: Its vs It's, Whose vs Who's, Plural vs Possessive

Learn apostrophe rules with examples — master its vs it's...

Core Governing Rule Formula:
Its = possessive. It's = it is / it has.

Learn apostrophe rules with examples, and you'll stop second-guessing yourself every time you write "its" or "it's." The apostrophe is a tiny mark, but it causes more writing errors than almost any other punctuation symbol. This guide covers every major rule — possession, contractions, plurals, and the three most confused pairs: its/it's, whose/who's, and plural/possessive nouns.

Why Apostrophes Confuse Even Good Writers

Apostrophes do two completely different jobs: they show possession (ownership) and they show contraction (missing letters). The confusion happens because English also uses "s" to mark plurals — so three different uses of "s" and apostrophes collide in everyday writing.

The 7 Core Apostrophe Rules

Rule 1: Singular Possessive — Add 's

For a singular noun, add an apostrophe + s to show ownership.

  • The dog's leash is red.
  • My sister's car is new.

Rule 2: Plural Nouns Ending in S — Add Only '

If the plural noun already ends in "s," just add an apostrophe after it.

  • The students' project won first prize.
  • The teachers' lounge is upstairs.

Rule 3: Irregular Plurals (Not Ending in S) — Add 's

Irregular plurals like "children," "men," "women" don't end in s, so they take 's.

  • The children's toys were scattered everywhere.
  • The women's team won the match.

Rule 4: Contractions — Apostrophe Replaces Missing Letters

An apostrophe in a contraction marks where letters have been removed.

  • do not → don't
  • she is → she's
  • they have → they've

Rule 5: Its vs It's

This is the single most common apostrophe mistake in English.

  • Its = possessive (belonging to it). No apostrophe.
    • The company increased its revenue this year.
  • It's = contraction of "it is" or "it has." Always has an apostrophe.
    • It's going to rain today. (It is going to rain.)
    • It's been a long week. (It has been a long week.)

Rule 6: Whose vs Who's

Same pattern as its/it's — possession vs. contraction.

  • Whose = possessive (belonging to whom). No apostrophe.
    • Whose laptop is this?
  • Who's = contraction of "who is" or "who has."
    • Who's coming to the meeting? (Who is coming?)
    • Who's finished the report? (Who has finished?)

Rule 7: No Apostrophe for Plain Plurals

Don't add an apostrophe just to make a word plural — this is one of the most common writing errors.

  • Wrong: The 1990's were a great decade.
  • Right: The 1990s were a great decade.
  • Wrong: I bought two laptop's today.
  • Right: I bought two laptops today.

Memory Trick: "Sam's Puppy Plays, Catching Its Wandering Nose"

This one sentence helps you recall all 7 rules in order:

  • Sam's → Singular possessive (add 's)
  • Puppy → Plural ending in s (add only ')
  • Plays → Plural irregular, not ending in s (add 's)
  • Catching → Contractions (apostrophe for missing letters)
  • Its → Its vs It's
  • Wandering → Whose vs Who's
  • Nose → No apostrophe for plain plurals

Read this sentence once before you start writing or taking a test — it takes 5 seconds and covers every major trap.

Plural vs Possessive: The Quick Test

Ask yourself: "Does this word own something, or is there just more than one?"

  • More than one → just add "s" (no apostrophe): books, cars, teachers
  • Owns something → add apostrophe: book's cover, car's engine, teacher's desk

If you can replace the word with "belonging to [noun]," it needs an apostrophe.

  • "The dog's bone" = "the bone belonging to the dog" → apostrophe needed.
  • "Two dogs ran" = just more than one dog → no apostrophe.

5 Common Apostrophe Mistakes

  1. Writing "it's" when you mean possession (its color, not it's color).
  2. Adding apostrophes to plain plurals (banana's instead of bananas).
  3. Confusing "whose" and "who's" in questions.
  4. Forgetting the apostrophe in contractions under time pressure (dont instead of don't).
  5. Placing the apostrophe incorrectly in plural possessives (childrens' instead of children's).

Practice Set: 6 Questions with Answers

Q1. Choose the correct option: "The cat chased ___ tail." (its / it's)
Answer: its — possessive, no apostrophe needed.

Q2. Choose the correct option: "___ going to the party tonight?" (Whose / Who's)
Answer: Who's — contraction of "who is."

Q3. Identify the error: "The dog's bowl's and the cat's toy's were everywhere."
Answer: "bowl's" and "toy's" should be plain plurals — "bowls" and "toys" (no apostrophe needed, since they're not possessive here).

Q4. Choose the correct option: "The company increased ___ profits." (its / it's)
Answer: its — possessive form.

Q5. Identify the error: "The childrens' books were on the shelf."
Answer: Should be "children's books" — "children" is already plural, so add 's, not s'.

Q6. Choose the correct option: "___ book is this on the table?" (Whose / Who's)
Answer: Whose — possessive, asking who owns the book.

FAQ

Q: What's the easiest way to remember its vs it's?
A: If you can replace the word with "it is" or "it has" and the sentence still makes sense, use "it's." Otherwise, use "its."

Q: Do plural nouns always need an apostrophe?
A: No. Plain plurals (more than one of something) never need an apostrophe — only possessive plurals do.

Q: Why do irregular plurals like "children's" take 's instead of just '?
A: Because "children" doesn't already end in "s," the normal possessive rule (add 's) applies, just like a singular noun.
