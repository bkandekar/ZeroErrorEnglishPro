Why Voice Questions Keep Costing Marks

The core idea is simple. The difficulty appears only when three things have to happen at the same time:

  • The object of the active sentence must become the subject of the passive sentence.
  • The verb must change into the correct form of “be + V3” according to the original tense.
  • The original subject is either dropped or placed after “by”, depending on whether it is important or known.

Under exam pressure most aspirants either change the tense incorrectly, forget the “being / been” forms, or keep an unnecessary “by” phrase. That is exactly what this guide is built to eliminate.

Complete Rules: Active → Passive Conversion

The basic formula never changes:

Object of Active + appropriate form of “be” + V3 (+ by + original subject)

Only transitive verbs (verbs that take an object) can be changed into Passive Voice. Intransitive verbs have no object, so they cannot form a passive sentence.

When to use Passive Voice in exams

  • The doer is unknown or unimportant.
  • The focus is on the action or the receiver of the action.
  • The sentence sounds more formal or objective (common in official notices and reports).

Special Cases and Named Traps

These are the traps that appear most frequently in SSC and Banking papers:

  • Trap 1 – Missing “being / been”: Present Continuous and Perfect forms are the most common error sources.
  • Trap 2 – Unnecessary “by” phrase: When the doer is unknown or obvious, the “by” phrase should be dropped.
  • Trap 3 – Wrong form of “be”: Matching the number and person of the new subject is mandatory.
  • Trap 4 – Imperative sentences: “Open the door” → “Let the door be opened.”
  • Trap 5 – Verbs with two objects: Either object can become the subject; both forms are usually accepted if grammar is correct.
  • Trap 6 – “Who” questions: “Who wrote this?” → “By whom was this written?”

How to Practise: The 4-Step Checking Method

  1. Identify whether the verb is transitive and locate the object.
  2. Change the object into the new subject and put the correct form of “be + V3”.
  3. Decide whether the original subject needs “by” or can be dropped.
  4. Verify tense consistency and subject-verb agreement with the new subject.

Practise this sequence on 10–15 sentences every day for two weeks. The recognition speed improves dramatically once the steps become automatic.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can every sentence be changed into Passive Voice?

A: No. Only sentences with transitive verbs (verbs that take an object) can be changed. Intransitive verbs have no object, so Passive Voice is not possible.

Q: Is the “by” phrase always necessary?

A: No. When the doer is unknown, unimportant or obvious from context, the “by” phrase is usually omitted.

Q: Which tenses cause the most errors in exams?

A: Present Continuous, Present Perfect and Past Continuous. The “being” and “been” forms are frequently missed.

Q: How should I practise for Error Spotting?

A: Take any active sentence, convert it correctly, then deliberately create the common wrong versions and practise spotting them under time limit.

Q: Is Active and Passive Voice asked in Banking exams as well?

A: Yes. The same rules apply. Banking papers also test voice through Error Spotting and Sentence Improvement.
