Collective Nouns and Verb Agreement: Team, Committee, Jury, Police – Singular or Plural?

Is it 'The committee has' or 'have'? Learn collective noun agreement with team, jury, police and more, plus practice questions for competitive exams.

Core Governing Rule Formula:
Group as ONE unit = singular verb + its | members acting SEPARATELY = plural verb + their.

Is it “The committee has decided” or “The committee have decided”? Open any newspaper and you'll find both. No wonder students feel confused, and no wonder examiners love the topic.

I've taught this rule for more than two decades, and I can tell you where the confusion starts. Students try to memorise a list: committee is singular, police is plural, jury is... both? A list will fail you, because the same noun can take a singular or a plural verb depending on what the sentence is saying.

In this guide, you'll learn one question that solves almost every collective noun trap. You'll also get the list of nouns that are always plural, the phrases that only look collective, what exams expect, and a ten-sentence practice set with answers.

What Is a Collective Noun?

A collective noun is a single word that names a group of persons, animals or things. The word is singular in form, but it points to many members.

Group of peopleGroup of animalsGroup of things
team, committee, juryherd, flock, packbunch, bundle, pile
family, class, staffswarm, pride, schoolset, collection, fleet
audience, crew, armypack of wolves, gagglepair, series, library

Because the word looks singular, you'd expect a singular verb every time. But the members inside the group can sometimes act on their own, and that's where the plural verb comes in.

The Golden Rule: Unit or Split

One Question to Ask
Is the group acting as ONE unit, or are the members acting SEPARATELY?
One unit = singular verb and its.
Separate members = plural verb and their.
Memory trick: UNIT or SPLIT
UNIT goes with its and a singular verb. SPLIT goes with their and a plural verb. If the members are split in opinion or action, go plural.

Here's how to decide in a few seconds. Look for clues in the sentence. If the group does something together, such as submitting a report, winning a match or announcing a decision, it's a unit. If the sentence mentions members disagreeing, voting differently or doing separate jobs, it's a split.

When the Group Acts as One Unit

Most exam sentences treat the group as one unit. Here are typical examples:

  • The committee has submitted its report.
  • The jury has given its verdict.
  • The team is celebrating its victory.
  • The class is writing a test today.
  • The army has crossed the border.

Notice the pattern. In each sentence, one action belongs to the whole group, and the pronoun its matches the singular verb. If you see a plural pronoun like their with a singular verb, you've found the error.

When the Members Act Separately

Now the other side. When the members of a group do separate things, or hold different views, the verb turns plural:

  • The jury are divided in their opinions.
  • The staff are busy with their own work.
  • The family are arguing among themselves.
  • The committee have failed to agree among themselves.

Clue words that signal a split include divided, disagree, among themselves, each, their own and separately. When you see one, switch to the plural verb.

Honestly, plural-verb sentences are rarer in exams than unit sentences. If a sentence doesn't give you a split clue, go with the singular.

The Pronoun Partner: Its or Their

The verb and the pronoun must agree with each other. A sentence cannot say “The committee has submitted their report”, because has is singular and their is plural. The correct version is “has submitted its report”.

WrongCorrect
The team has won their match.The team has won its match.
The jury have given its verdict.The jury has given its verdict.
The staff is busy with its own work.The staff are busy with their own work.

Many error spotting questions hide the mistake right here. The verb looks fine, but the pronoun in the next part doesn't match. Always read both parts of the sentence before you decide.

Nouns That Are Always Plural

Some nouns look singular but are always plural, and they never switch. These are the favourite exam traps in this topic.

NounExample
PoliceThe police have arrested the thief.
CattleThe cattle are grazing in the field.
PeoplePeople are waiting outside.
PoultryThe poultry are kept in the shed.
ClergyThe clergy are invited to the ceremony.
GentryThe gentry were present at the ball.

If you need to talk about one policeman, use “a police officer”. Never say “a police”. And for one person, use “a person” instead of “a people”. Note that peoples (with an s) means nations or races, and it's rarely tested.

Phrases That Look Collective But Aren't

Some phrases look collective but are built with a different structure, and the rule changes.

  1. A group of / a flock of / a bunch of + plural noun. Here the subject is the first noun. “A flock of birds is flying.” “A bunch of keys is missing.” Cover the of-phrase, and the verb is obvious.
  2. A pair of / a set of / a series of. The verb follows pair, set or series: “A pair of shoes is on the shelf.”
  3. The majority of + plural noun. When the members are meant, use plural: “The majority of the students are present.” When the whole majority acts as a unit, use singular: “The majority has decided.”

If these patterns look familiar, that's because they're related to the subject-hiding trap I explained in the 15 mistakes that cost marks in SSC CGL. The word you need is often one step before the plural noun.

British and American Usage: What Exams Expect

Here's something worth knowing. British English often treats collective nouns as plural when the members matter, as in “The team are playing well.” American English almost always uses the singular: “The team is playing well.”

Indian competitive exams follow formal grammar books, and most answer keys prefer the singular with a unit meaning. So here's the safe strategy:

  • No clue of splitting? Use the singular verb with its.
  • A clue like divided, disagree or among themselves? Use the plural verb with their.
  • The noun is police, cattle, people or poultry? Always plural.

Four Sentences, Four Decisions

Let's practise the golden question on four fresh sentences, so you can see how the decision works in real time.

  1. The board has approved its annual budget. The board approves as one body. Unit. Singular verb, its.
  2. The board are expected to arrive at different times. Different times means separate arrivals. Split. Plural verb.
  3. The crew is ready for takeoff. The whole crew is prepared together. Unit. Singular.
  4. The crew have taken their positions. Each member goes to a position of their own. Split. Plural. Here the grammar is correct, but exams rarely test this.

Notice what changed between sentences one and two: not the noun, but the action. That's the real skill. You don't memorise nouns, you read the action.

5 Mistakes Students Make With Collective Nouns

  1. Memorising a fixed list. Lists help with police and cattle, but a list can't tell you about team or jury. Read the action instead.
  2. Fixing the verb and forgetting the pronoun. Students correct the verb and leave their in the next part. Always check the pronoun.
  3. Using the plural because the noun means many people. The idea of many people doesn't change the grammar. A singular noun takes a singular verb unless the split clue appears.
  4. Treating police as singular. Police is always plural. If you see “The police has”, it's an error.
  5. Ignoring the head noun. In “A flock of sheep is”, the subject is flock. The plural sheep is a decoy.

If you fix these five habits, you'll answer collective noun questions faster and with more confidence than most aspirants.

Using Collective Nouns Correctly in Your Own Writing

These rules don't only help you in error spotting. They also protect your marks in essays, letters and comprehension answers, where a mismatched pronoun makes a good paragraph look careless.

Here's a simple habit for your own writing. Decide at the start of the sentence whether the group is a unit or a split, then stay with that choice until the full stop. If you begin with “The committee has...”, finish with “...its decision”. Never switch halfway.

Mixed (wrong)Consistent (right)
The team has played well, and they will win.The team has played well, and it will win.
The class is silent because they are writing.The class is silent because it is writing.
The jury has retired, but they cannot agree.The jury have retired, but they cannot agree.

The last row is interesting. The members cannot agree, which is a split clue, so the plural verb from the start keeps the whole sentence consistent. Choose your side early, and the rest follows.

When you review your own paragraphs, scan every collective noun and check the verb and pronoun that follow it. It takes ten seconds, and it removes an error that many writers never notice.

Case Study: Two Questions, Two Different Errors

Here are two SSC CGL exam pattern practice questions. Each hides a different error.

Question 1
The jury (A) / have given (B) / its verdict. (C) / No error (D)

Ask the golden question. The jury gives one verdict, so it acts as a unit. The verb should be has given. The error is in B.

Question 2
The committee has submitted (A) / their report (B) / to the chairman. (C) / No error (D)

The verb has is singular, so the pronoun must be its. The error is in B, and the corrected form is “its report”. See how the verb part looked perfect? That's exactly where students go wrong.

Practice: Spot the Error

Find the error in each sentence before you read the answers. These are practice sentences in the common exam pattern, not year-tagged past questions.

  1. The police has arrested two suspects.
  2. The committee have submitted their report.
  3. The cattle is grazing in the field.
  4. The jury was divided in their opinions.
  5. The team is celebrating its victory. (Check if there is an error.)
  6. The audience were large this evening.
  7. A flock of sheep are crossing the road.
  8. The staff is arguing among themselves.
  9. My family is planning to sell their house.
  10. The people was angry about the decision.
Answers
  1. “has” should be have.
  2. “have” should be has, and “their” should be its.
  3. “is” should be are.
  4. “was” should be were. The members are divided.
  5. No error. The team acts as one unit.
  6. “were” should be was. Size describes the audience as a unit.
  7. “are” should be is. The subject is flock.
  8. “is” should be are. Members argue among themselves.
  9. “their” should be its. The family acts as one unit.
  10. “was” should be were. People is always plural.

4 Tips From the Classroom

Tip 1: Look for clue words

Circle words like divided, among themselves and their own. They tell you the members are acting separately.

Tip 2: Check the pronoun in the next part

Always read the second part of the sentence. Its, their, itself and themselves reveal whether the writer treated the group as one or many.

Tip 3: Memorise the four plural nouns

Police, cattle, people, poultry. Say them aloud five times, and you'll never lose a mark on them again.

Tip 4: Cover the of-phrase

With a flock of, a pair of or a set of, cover the of-phrase and match the verb to the first noun.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is “audience” singular or plural?
Treat it as singular when it acts together: “The audience was large.” Use plural only when the sentence shows individuals acting separately, as in “The audience were clapping and cheering in their seats”.

Why is “police” always plural?
It names a body of officers and is always used with a plural verb: “The police are investigating.”

Should I use “its” or “their” with a collective noun?
Match the verb. A singular verb takes its, and a plural verb takes their.

What about “a group of students”?
The subject is group, so exams expect a singular verb: “A group of students is waiting.”

Is “government” singular or plural?
In exams, treat it as singular: “The government has announced its policy.” British writing sometimes uses plural, but answer keys follow the unit meaning.

Is “the committee have decided” wrong?
In British writing it can appear, but exams treat a committee that decides as a unit and prefer “has decided”.

How many collective noun questions can I expect?
It varies from paper to paper, but the rule often appears inside error spotting and sentence improvement questions, so it's worth mastering.

Final word. Ask one question: is the group acting as one unit, or as separate members? That single question solves most of the topic.
