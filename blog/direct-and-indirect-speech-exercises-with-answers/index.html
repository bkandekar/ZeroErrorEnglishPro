Direct and Indirect Speech Exercises with Answers for SSC CGL, Bank PO and CUET

Practise 44 direct and indirect speech exercises with answers and reasons for SSC CGL, bank PO and CUET, plus memory tricks, MCQs and a 7-day plan.

Core Governing Rule Formula:
P-T-P | S-O-N | ONE STEP BACK | IF for yes/no, WH stays | COMMAND = person plus TO

Rules teach you what to do. Practice teaches you to do it under pressure. That's why the students who improve fastest in direct and indirect speech are not the ones who read the most posts. They're the ones who convert the most sentences, check every answer and learn from every mistake.

This post gives you exactly that: 44 exercises with answers, built for SSC CGL, bank PO, CUET and other competitive exam aspirants. The questions are grouped into six sets: statements, questions, commands and requests, exclamations and wishes, multiple-choice questions and reverse conversion from indirect to direct speech. Every answer has a short reason. In the middle, there's a memory-tricks cheat sheet that gathers every shortcut from this series in one place.

A note on the questions: they're original sentences on common exam patterns, not questions copied from any particular year's paper. If you need to revise the rules first, start with my post on direct and indirect speech rules with examples.

How to use this practice set:
  1. Keep a notebook and a pen. Write every answer, even if you think you know it.
  2. As a rule of thumb, give each conversion about 60 seconds. Speed will come later.
  3. Don't look at the answer key until you've finished a whole set.
  4. For every mistake, write the sentence, the correct answer and the rule in a few words.
  5. Retake the questions you got wrong after three days.

Set A: Statements (10 questions)

Convert each sentence into indirect speech. Use the P-T-P check: Person, Time, Place.

  1. Rahul said, "I am preparing for the exam."
  2. She said to me, "I will send you the file tomorrow."
  3. The teacher said, "The earth moves round the sun."
  4. My brother said, "I have bought a new phone."

Memory tricks cheat sheet (screenshot this)

Before you attempt the remaining sets, spend two minutes with this table. It gathers every shortcut from this series, in one place.

What to rememberMemory shortcut
Order of checksP-T-P: Person, Time, Place.
PronounsS-O-N: first person follows Subject, second person follows Object, third person No change.
Near and far wordsN-F: Near words become Far words (now, then; here, there).
Yesterday and tomorrowY-B, T-A: yesterday = before, tomorrow = after.
TensesONE STEP BACK, like reversing a car.
Simple, continuous, perfectSAME FAMILY, PAST ADDRESS.
Past tensesPAST GOES PERFECT: add "had".
ModalsC-M-W: Can to Could, May to Might, Will to Would.
No changeFrozen facts don't move. Present stays present (says, will say).
Reporting verbsMatch the MOOD. PORAT verbs take to + verb. SAD verbs take -ing.
QuestionsIF for yes/no, WH stays. KILL THE Q: no ?, no do/does/did, subject first.
CommandsCOMMAND = person plus TO. Negative: person plus not to.
ExclamationsFEEL the interjection, VERY the rest.

Set E: Multiple-choice questions (8 questions)

Choose the correct indirect speech. This is the format you'll see in many objective exams.

  1. He said, "I am going to Delhi tomorrow."
    (a) He said that he was going to Delhi tomorrow.
    (b) He said that he is going to Delhi the next day.
    (c) He said that he was going to Delhi the next day.
    (d) He said that he had gone to Delhi the next day.
  2. She said to me, "Where do you live?"
    (a) She asked me where did I live.
    (b) She asked me where I lived.
    (c) She told me where I lived.
    (d) She asked me that where I lived.
  3. The teacher said, "Honesty is the best policy."
    (a) The teacher said that honesty was the best policy.
    (b) The teacher said that honesty is the best policy.
    (c) The teacher said that honesty had been the best policy.
    (d) The teacher said that honesty would be the best policy.
  4. He said to me, "Please help me."
    (a) He requested me to help him.
    (b) He ordered me to help him.
    (c) He said me to help him.
    (d) He requested that help him.
  5. She said, "Alas! I have failed."
    (a) She exclaimed with sorrow that she had failed.
    (b) She said alas that she failed.
    (c) She exclaimed that alas she had failed.
    (d) She cried that she has failed.
  6. Ravi said to Sunil, "I will help you."
    (a) Ravi told Sunil that I would help you.
    (b) Ravi told Sunil that he would help him.
    (c) Ravi said Sunil that he will help him.
    (d) Ravi asked Sunil that he would help him.
  7. The boy said, "I saw her yesterday."
    (a) The boy said that he saw her yesterday.
    (b) The boy said that he saw her the previous day.
    (c) The boy said that he had seen her the previous day.
    (d) The boy said that he has seen her the day before.
  8. He said to her, "Do you know the way?"
    (a) He asked her if she knew the way.
    (b) He asked her do she know the way.
    (c) He said to her if she knew the way.
    (d) He asked her that she knew the way.

Set F: Reverse conversion, indirect to direct (4 questions)

Now go the other way. Convert each indirect sentence back into direct speech. Reverse the rules: step the tense forward, restore the pronouns and bring back the near words.

  1. She said that she was tired.
  2. He told me that he would call me the next day.
  3. The teacher asked the boy why he was late.
  4. She requested him to open the window.

Answer key with reasons

Set A
1) Rahul said that he was preparing for the exam. (present continuous to past continuous)
2) She told me that she would send me the file the next day. (said to to told, will to would, tomorrow to the next day)
3) The teacher said that the earth moves round the sun. (universal truth: no change)
4) My brother said that he had bought a new phone. (present perfect to past perfect)
5) They said that they had gone to Goa the previous month. (past simple to past perfect, last month to the previous month)
6) The manager told us that we must finish the work that day. (must stays, today to that day)
7) Sneha said that her sister was coming there then. (here to there, now to then)
8) He says that he likes this movie. (present reporting verb: no change)
9) Anita told her friend that she had been reading a novel the previous night. (past continuous to past perfect continuous)
10) The boys said that they could solve that problem. (can to could, this to that)

Keep an error log (five minutes a day)

Every wrong answer is a free lesson. In your notebook, make three columns: the question number, your answer and the correct answer with a few words on the rule. For example: "Q14 | asked Sohan if he will join | would join, tense steps back".

Read the log once a week. You'll see that the same three or four mistakes keep coming back, and those are the ones to fix first. This works far better than doing a hundred new questions without looking back.

Frequently asked questions

How many direct and indirect speech questions should I practise every day?

Ten is a good number. Solve them, check the answers and note your mistakes. Ten careful conversions a day teach you more than forty rushed ones.

Should I write the answers or just think them through?

Write them. Writing exposes small errors, like a missing "that" or a wrong pronoun, which you would miss while thinking. In the exam, you'll have to write or mark answers anyway.

Can there be more than one correct answer?

Yes, in some cases. For example, "told" and "ordered" can both work for a command, and "the next day" and "the following day" are both correct for "tomorrow". Exam answer keys follow the standard rule, so stick to the rules in this series.

How long should 40 questions take?

As a rule of thumb, about 40 to 50 minutes at the start. With practice, the time comes down, but accuracy matters more than speed in the first two weeks.

Why do some answers keep the present tense?

Because the tense doesn't change when the reporting verb is in the present or future, or when the statement is a universal truth. Check the exceptions in my post on the tense changes chart.

What should I do if I keep making the same mistake?

Go back to the memory trick for that rule, say it aloud, and write five new sentences using it. Then retake the questions you got wrong after three days.

Practice makes the pattern permanent

Forty-four questions are only the start. Retake this set in three days and again in a week. By the third attempt, most of these conversions will feel automatic.
