Reporting Verbs List for Indirect Speech: Said, Told, Asked, Advised with Examples

Learn the reporting verbs list for indirect speech: said, told, asked, advised and more, with patterns, memory tricks, 12 examples and a practice set.

Core Governing Rule Formula:
Match the MOOD | PORAT + to-verb | SAD verbs take -ING | Person plus TO

Read this sentence: He said to me, "Do not touch it." Now report it. Many students write: "He said me not to touch it." It looks close, but it's wrong in two ways. "Said me" is not correct English, and the word "said" doesn't match the mood of the sentence at all. The speaker wasn't just talking. He was warning me.

The correct answer is: He warned me not to touch it.

That one word, the reporting verb, carries the speaker's mood, and examiners love to test whether you can choose it. Using "said" for everything isn't wrong in every case, but it often costs marks, especially in questions about requests, advice, warnings, suggestions and exclamations.

In this guide, I'll give you a complete list of reporting verbs grouped by their purpose, the four patterns that follow them, memory tricks that make them stick, the mistakes examiners love and a practice set with answers. What is a reporting verb?

The reporting verb is the verb in the first part of the sentence, the part that tells us who spoke and how. In the sentence He said, "I am tired," the reporting verb is said. In indirect speech it becomes He said that he was tired.

Two things depend on the reporting verb. First, the tense change: if the reporting verb is in the past, the tense steps back. Second, the pattern that follows: some verbs take "that", some take "to" and some take "-ing". Choosing the right verb gives you the right meaning and the right pattern at the same time.

Memory trick 1: Match the MOOD

The easiest way to pick a reporting verb is to ask yourself one question: what is the speaker doing? Is the speaker informing, asking, ordering, begging, advising, warning or suggesting? The answer is your verb.

Memory shortcut: Match the MOOD. Read the feeling of the sentence before you read its words. Information becomes said or told. A question becomes asked. A command becomes ordered. A polite request becomes requested. A suggestion that the speaker also joins becomes suggested.

The four patterns that follow reporting verbs

Choosing the verb is half the battle. The other half is the pattern that follows it. There are four patterns, and every reporting verb in the list belongs to one of them.

PatternExampleVerbs
Verb + that + clauseHe said that he was tired.said, told (+ person), explained, replied, announced, admitted, complained
Verb + person + to + verbHe advised me to rest.ordered, requested, advised, warned, begged, forbade, permitted
Verb + to + verbHe promised to help.promised, offered, refused, agreed, threatened
Verb + -ingHe denied stealing the money.denied, admitted, suggested
Memory shortcut: PORAT. Promise, Offer, Refuse, Agree, Threaten all take to + verb: he promised to help, she refused to pay.
Memory shortcut: SAD verbs take -ING. Suggest, Admit, Deny: he suggested going, she admitted breaking it, he denied taking it.
Memory shortcut: Orders, Advice and Warnings need a person plus TO. He ordered me to go. She advised him to wait. They warned us to be careful.

Memory trick 2: The prepositions that follow some verbs

A few reporting verbs take a preposition, and a wrong one costs marks. Learn them in pairs.

VerbPrepositionExample
apologisedto (person) for (thing)He apologised to me for the delay.
thanked(person) for (thing)She thanked me for my help.
congratulated(person) on (thing)He congratulated me on my success.
accused(person) of (thing)They accused him of cheating.
insistedonShe insisted on paying the bill.
blamed(person) for (thing)He blamed me for the mistake.
Memory shortcut: Podium ON, favour FOR. On a podium, we congratulate: congratulate ON. For a favour, we thank: thank FOR. More pairs like these are in my guide to the appropriate prepositions list.

How to choose the reporting verb in 3 steps

  1. Read the mood. Is the speaker informing, asking, ordering, requesting, advising, warning, suggesting, promising or thanking?
  2. Check the pattern. Does that verb take "that", "person + to", "to" or "-ing"? Write the sentence in that pattern.
  3. Apply the other changes. Move the tense one step back, and change the pronouns, time and place words. If you need a refresher, see my posts on the tense changes chart and on pronouns, time and place words.

7 common mistakes with reporting verbs

WrongRightWhy
He said me not to go.He told me not to go. / He advised me not to go.Said to a person becomes told, and a mood verb is even better.
She suggested me to rest.She suggested that I should rest.Suggest takes "that" or -ing, not person + to.
He denied to take the money.He denied taking the money.Deny takes -ing.
The boy admitted to have broken the glass.The boy admitted breaking the glass.Admit takes -ing or "that".
He apologised me for the delay.He apologised to me for the delay.Apologise needs "to" before the person.
She congratulated him for his success.She congratulated him on his success.Congratulate takes "on".
He promised that he helps me.He promised to help me.Promise takes "to + verb", or "that" with would.

Practice set: 12 questions

  1. He said to me, "Please lend me your pen."
  2. The teacher said to the class, "Do not talk in the library."
  3. My friend said to me, "You should join a coaching class."
  4. Rita said to her mother, "I am sorry for my mistake."
  5. He said, "I did not break the window."
  6. Meena said, "Let us visit the museum."
  7. She said to him, "Thank you for the gift."
  8. The officer said to the guard, "Open the gate."
  9. He said to me, "I will drop you at the station."
  10. The girl said, "Alas! I have lost my bag."

Answers:
1) He requested me to lend him my pen.
2) The teacher ordered (or told) the class not to talk in the library.
3) My friend advised me to join a coaching class.
4) Rita apologised to her mother for her mistake.
5) He denied breaking the window.
6) Meena suggested that they should visit the museum.
7) She thanked him for the gift.
8) The officer ordered the guard to open the gate.
9) He promised to drop me at the station.
10) The girl exclaimed with sorrow that she had lost her bag.

Frequently asked questions

What is a reporting verb?

A reporting verb is the verb in the reporting clause, the part that tells us who spoke and how. Examples are said, told, asked, ordered, advised and suggested. It decides the mood and the pattern of the indirect sentence.

What is the difference between "said" and "told"?

"Said" is used without a listener, as in "He said that he was busy". "Told" always needs a listener right after it, as in "He told me that he was busy". "He said me" and "He told that" are both wrong.

Which reporting verb should I use for questions?

Use asked or enquired, and sometimes wondered or demanded. After the verb, write the question in normal word order, with no question mark, and use "if" or "whether" for yes or no questions.

What is the difference between "advised" and "ordered"?

"Advised" is for suggestions that the listener may or may not follow, as in "You should take rest". "Ordered" is for commands that must be followed, as in "Fire!" or "Do not leave". Choose by the force of the sentence.

Which reporting verbs take -ing?

Suggest, admit and deny are the main ones. Remember them as the SAD verbs: he suggested going, she admitted breaking it, he denied taking it. Verbs that take a preposition first, such as insist on, also take -ing: she insisted on paying.

How can I remember so many reporting verbs?

Do not learn them as a long list. Learn them by mood and by pattern: Match the MOOD, PORAT, SAD and person plus TO. Practise ten sentences a day for a week, and the verbs become natural.
