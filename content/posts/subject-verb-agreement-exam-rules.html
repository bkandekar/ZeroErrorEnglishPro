Every competitive exam aspirant knows the kindergarten formula: a singular subject takes a singular verb, and a plural subject takes a plural verb. Yet year after year, in exams like SSC CGL Tier 1 & Tier 2, IBPS PO, SBI Clerk, and CDS, Subject-Verb Agreement questions account for nearly 25% of all negative marks in the English section.

The reason is simple: examiners never test you on simple sentences. You will never see "The boy run" in a Tier-2 paper. Instead, examiners construct sentences with long prepositional intervening clauses, compound impostors like "along with" or "as well as", and misleading relative pronoun pivots. Your ear listens to the word right before the verb—and that word is almost always a carefully placed trap.

The Core Concept in Plain English

In English grammar, a verb must agree strictly and solely with its true grammatical head subject. No matter how much descriptive baggage, parenthetical commentary, or prepositional clutter the examiner stuffs between that subject and the verb, the grammatical contract does not change.

The 4 Governing Rules You Must Memorize

Rule 1: The Intervening Modifier Principle

Words introduced by with, along with, together with, as well as, accompanied by, in addition to, besides, like, unlike are parenthetical prepositions—not coordinating conjunctions. They do not turn a singular subject into a compound plural.

Formula: Subject 1 + [as well as / along with / together with + Noun 2] + Verb (agrees strictly with Subject 1)

Rule 2: The Proximity Principle with Correlative Conjunctions

When two subjects are connected by either... or, neither... nor, or, nor, or not only... but also, the verb agrees with the subject that sits closest to it.

Formula: Neither + Subject 1 + nor + Subject 2 + Verb (agrees with Subject 2)

Rule 3: The Distributive Singularity Rule

The words each, every, neither, either, one of, everyone, someone, nobody are grammatically singular distributives. Even when followed by an "of + plural noun" phrase, the verb remains singular.

Formula: Each of / Neither of / One of + Plural Noun + Singular Verb

Rule 4: The Relative Pronoun Antecedent Pivot

In "one of the + plural noun + who/that...", the relative pronoun refers to the plural noun antecedent, so the verb is plural. BUT if preceded by "the only", the verb reverts to singular.

Formula A: One of the [Plural Noun] + who/that + Plural Verb
Formula B: The ONLY one of the [Plural Noun] + who/that + Singular Verb

4 Worked Exam-Style Examples

Incorrect

The captain, along with all the members of the rescue squad, were awarded national medals for gallantry.

Correct

The captain, along with all the members of the rescue squad, was awarded national medals for gallantry.

Examiner Trap Analysis: The true subject is "The captain" (singular). The phrase "along with all the members..." is a prepositional modifier and does not create a plural compound subject.
Incorrect

Neither the principal nor the senior faculty lecturers was present at the academic symposium.

Correct

Neither the principal nor the senior faculty lecturers were present at the academic symposium.

Examiner Trap Analysis: In neither... nor, the verb agrees with the closer subject. "Senior faculty lecturers" is plural and sits adjacent to the verb slot, mandating were.
Incorrect

Each of the ten shortlisted finalists have demonstrated exceptional analytical proficiency.

Correct

Each of the ten shortlisted finalists has demonstrated exceptional analytical proficiency.

Examiner Trap Analysis: The plural noun "finalists" is the object of preposition "of". The subject is "Each", which takes the singular verb has.
Incorrect

Dr. Roy is the only one of the lead researchers who have successfully synthesized the new polymer.

Correct

Dr. Roy is the only one of the lead researchers who has successfully synthesized the new polymer.

Examiner Trap Analysis: Notice the magic modifier "the only". Without "the only", the verb would be plural (have); but "the only one" forces the entire relative clause to focus on Dr. Roy alone.

Two Real Exam Traps from SSC & Banking Papers

Exam Trap 1 — SSC CGL Tier 2

The Inverted Subject Trap

Examiners invert the normal subject-verb order using negative adverbs or directional phrases like "Hardly", "Scarcely", "Under the bridge", "Beyond the mountains".

Around the corner lies three newly established bank branches.

Around the corner lie three newly established bank branches.

The subject is "three bank branches" (plural), not "the corner".

Exam Trap 2 — IBPS PO Mains

The Noun of Multitude Division

Collective nouns (committee, jury, board, army, government) take singular verbs when acting as a unified whole, but plural verbs when members act in discord or carry distinct opinions.

The jury was divided in their opinions regarding the forensic evidence.

The jury were divided in their opinions regarding the forensic evidence.

The pronoun "their" and word "divided" signal individual members in conflict.

The 15-Second Exam Shortcut: The Prepositional Blindfold

Mental Speed Technique

The Prepositional Blindfold Rule

Whenever you encounter an error-detection sentence longer than 15 words, put a mental blindfold over every prepositional phrase starting with of, in, at, on, with, along with, as well as, accompanied by, between, and for until you reach the finite verb.

"The quality [of these newly procured fabrics imported from Europe] (is / are) exceptional."
Blindfold the brackets → "The quality is exceptional." Solved in 3 seconds.

Practice Questions with Step-by-Step Explanations

Q1: Find the error part: The introduction of cashless transactions, digital wallets, and unified payments (A) / have revolutionized small-scale commerce (B) / in rural districts. (C) / No error (D) Show Answer

Correct Answer: (B)

Explanation: The subject is the singular noun "The introduction". The items "of cashless transactions, digital wallets..." form a prepositional phrase modifying introduction. Replace the plural auxiliary "have" with the singular auxiliary "has".

Q2: Find the error part: Neither the presiding magistrate nor the court clerks (A) / was willing to postpone the trial hearings (B) / past the statutory deadline. (C) / No error (D) Show Answer

Correct Answer: (B)

Explanation: In neither... nor, the verb must agree with the closer subject. Here, the closer subject is "the court clerks" (plural). Therefore, replace the singular "was" with the plural "were".

Q3: Find the error part: Ananya is the only one of the participating delegates (A) / who have presented a thoroughly verified empirical model (B) / at the global summit. (C) / No error (D) Show Answer

Correct Answer: (B)

Explanation: Because the phrase is modified by "the only one", the relative clause applies strictly to Ananya (singular). Replace "who have presented" with "who has presented".

Q4: Find the error part: A consignment of eighty metric tons of food grains, (A) / along with relief kits and blankets, (B) / were dispatched to the flood victims. (C) / No error (D) Show Answer

Correct Answer: (C)

Explanation: The grammatical head subject is "A consignment" (singular). "Along with relief kits..." is parenthetical. Replace "were dispatched" with "was dispatched".

Frequently Asked Aspirant Questions (FAQ)

Q: How do I handle percentages, fractions, and proportions?

Unlike typical nouns, percentages and fractions (e.g. "half of", "two-thirds of", "40% of") take their verb from the noun in the 'of' phrase. If the noun is uncountable (e.g. "40% of the land is arable"), the verb is singular. If the noun is plural countable (e.g. "40% of the students are enrolled"), the verb is plural.

Q: What is the exact difference between "A number of" and "The number of"?

"A number of" means "many" and takes a plural verb (e.g. "A number of applicants have cleared Tier 1"). In contrast, "The number of" refers to the singular numerical total itself and takes a singular verb (e.g. "The number of vacancies has decreased this session").

Q: Does informal English allow plural verbs after "Neither of"?

In conversational British or American English, you may hear "Neither of them were there". However, competitive exam bodies (SSC, IBPS, UPSC) strictly penalize this. In competitive exams, neither of is non-negotiably singular.
