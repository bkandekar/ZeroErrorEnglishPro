You open the English section feeling confident. Then a long sentence appears and the question asks: “Identify the clause.” Your mind freezes. You know the definitions, but under the clock you still mark the wrong option.

Most aspirants lose 3–5 marks only because they look at the first word (who, that, when) instead of checking what the clause is actually doing in the sentence. Examiners know this weakness and place these traps in almost every SSC CGL, CHSL and IBPS paper.

The Core Concept: Three Jobs, Three Clauses

Think of clauses as workers with different job descriptions:

Noun Clause

Acts as a noun — subject, object or complement. Answers “What?” or “Who?”

I know that he is honest.

Adjective Clause

Describes a noun or pronoun. Answers “Which one?” or “What kind of?”

The man who came yesterday is my uncle.

Adverb Clause

Modifies a verb, adjective or whole idea. Answers When / Where / Why / How / Condition.

He left because he was tired.

The Definitive Identification Matrix

Type Common Starters Job in the Sentence Exam Trap Level
Noun Clause that, what, whether, if, how, why, where Subject / Object / Complement HIGH
Adjective Clause who, whom, whose, which, that, where Describes a noun / pronoun CRITICAL
Adverb Clause because, although, if, when, while, since, unless, as Modifies verb / shows time, reason, condition HIGH

4 Worked Exam-Style Examples

Incorrect

I know the boy who is standing there. → Adverb clause

Correct

I know the boy who is standing there → Adjective clause (describes “the boy”)

Examiner Trap Analysis: “Who” is a classic relative pronoun. The clause answers “Which boy?” and modifies the noun. It is not telling when or why.
Incorrect

What he said was true. → Adjective clause

Correct

What he said was true → Noun clause (acts as the subject)

Examiner Trap Analysis: The clause answers “What was true?” and functions as the subject of the sentence. Noun clauses often begin with what / that / how.
Incorrect

He left when the rain started. → Adjective clause

Correct

He left when the rain started → Adverb clause (answers “When?”)

Examiner Trap Analysis: The clause modifies the verb “left” and tells the time. Even though “when” can also introduce adjective clauses, here it is purely adverbial.
Incorrect

I know where he lives. → Adverb clause

Correct

I know where he lives → Noun clause (object of “know”)

Examiner Trap Analysis: Compare with “I know the place where he lives” (Adjective). Without a noun before “where”, the clause itself becomes the object → Noun clause.

Two Real Exam Traps from SSC & Banking Papers

Exam Trap 1 — SSC CGL

The “Where / When” Dual Role

The same word can introduce different clauses. Always check function, never only the first word.

I know where he lives → Adverb

I know where he lives → Noun clause (object)

The house where he lives is old → Adjective clause

Exam Trap 2 — IBPS / SBI

The Silent “That”

Many noun clauses drop “that”. Students miss them completely.

I believe he is innocent → No clause

I believe (that) he is innocent → Noun clause

The 10-Second Shortcut: The Question Test

Mental Speed Technique

The Question Test

Ask these three questions about the subordinate clause:
1. Does it answer “What?” or “Who?” → Noun Clause
2. Does it answer “Which one?” or “What kind of?” → Adjective Clause
3. Does it answer “When / Where / Why / How / Condition?” → Adverb Clause

Extra check: Remove the clause. If a noun loses its description → Adjective. If a verb loses its detail → Adverb. If the sentence loses a whole idea that was acting as subject/object → Noun.

Practice Questions with Step-by-Step Explanations

**Summary:** Q1: Identify the clause: “That she will win is certain.” Show Answer

Correct Answer: Noun Clause

Explanation: The clause “That she will win” is the subject of the sentence. It answers “What is certain?”

**Summary:** Q2: Identify the clause: “The book which you gave me is excellent.” Show Answer

Correct Answer: Adjective Clause

Explanation: “Which you gave me” describes the noun “book” and answers “Which book?”

**Summary:** Q3: Identify the clause: “Although it was raining, we went out.” Show Answer

Correct Answer: Adverb Clause

Explanation: The clause shows the condition/concession under which the action happened. It modifies the main verb “went”.

**Summary:** Q4: Identify the clause: “I don’t know how she managed it.” Show Answer

Correct Answer: Noun Clause

Explanation: “How she managed it” is the object of the verb “know”. It answers “What don’t I know?”

Frequently Asked Aspirant Questions (FAQ)

Q: Can the same word introduce different types of clauses?

Yes. “Where”, “when”, “that” and “if” can introduce Noun, Adjective or Adverb clauses. Always decide by the job the clause is doing, never only by the first word.

Q: How do I identify a clause when “that” is missing?

If a group of words has its own subject + verb and is acting as subject or object of the main verb, it is still a Noun clause even without “that” (e.g. “I think she is right”).

Q: Is this method enough for SSC CGL Tier-1 and Bank exams?

Yes. The Question Test + function check is exactly what high scorers use under time pressure. Combined with 15–20 timed practice questions daily for 10 days, accuracy becomes automatic.
